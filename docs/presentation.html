Pallet Polka

The development timeline of a top-down, keyboard-only forklift puzzle
21 October – 12 November 2026 · 617 commits · vanilla JS + Canvas 2D · no build, no dependencies

The idea & the stack

  • Stack coloured boxes in matching drop zones, then park the forklift
  • Rear-wheel-steering bicycle model: the tail swings out
  • Deterministic seeded level generator (mulberry32)
  • DOM-free game logic, so the tests run in plain Node
  • Static files on GitHub Pages (and an installable PWA); Firefox is the primary target, WebGL2 for the 3D views

Day 1 · 21 Oct · Morning

Core mechanics: forks & stacks

  • First playable browser game: forklift, boxes, zones, levels
  • Fork heights went from half-steps to whole levels 0–5
  • Tines never collide; the body and carried stack do
  • Lowering a carried stack releases it at resting height
  • Boxes rotate with the forklift and remember their rotation
Day 1 · 21 Oct · Midday

Rules, scoring & branding

  • Per-box scoring: crash and squareness deductions (5° allowance)
  • Parking spot; finishing needs parking and ≥ 50% score
  • Procedural audio: engine whine, music, fork sounds, success chime
  • Rebrand to “Forklift Frenzy”: splash screen, favicon, 3D boxes
  • Custom level seeds and a random-seed button
Day 1 · 21 Oct · Afternoon & evening

Cabin view, tutorial & high scores

  • Polygon-projected first-person view with forks, pallets, zones and parking
  • Camera follows fork height; front and rear top-down look windows
  • Nudging a box slowly squares it to the forklift
  • Local high scores per level and seed, with a time penalty
  • Step-by-step, action-dismissed tutorial; pause on lost focus
Day 3 · 23 Oct

Physics depth & new levels

  • Hard mode: pallets glide, split or get pushed; tines hit pallet runners
  • Boxes need 50% support or fall beside the stack; crooked towers
  • Rooms-and-corridors level generator replaces random pillars
  • Escape menu with full screen, mute, go-to-level and shortcuts
  • Floating damage popups and a break sound
Day 4 · 24 Oct

Handling & factory atmosphere

  • Height-based speed limit for raised forks; larger, speed-tapered steering lock
  • Overview becomes the default view and is remembered
  • Floor and wall decals: tire marks, yellow markings, windows, doors, signs
  • Ceiling, factory lights and fans; walls raised to clear a 5-box stack
  • Box decals and a ?debug overlay
Day 5 · 25 Oct

Sound, saves & touch

  • Reversing beep and a blue reversing light that walls cut off
  • Selectable MIDI music (public domain / CC0) with a drum synth; wall bonk and scrape sounds
  • Progress saved in localStorage; export/import of level state
  • Wall-mounted charger to park against; carried stacks can't be lifted into the ceiling
  • On-screen touch controls and an installable PWA
  • First version of this presentation
Days 6–7 · 26 – 27 Oct

On the phone

  • Rotatable landscape layout: field on top, steering bottom left, drive/forks bottom right, minimap and status between
  • Zoomed follow-camera overview; the map pans past its edges so pads never hide it
  • Brake removed (engine braking does the slowing); touch start enters full screen
  • Touch state derived from live touches (fixes flaky PWA steering); service worker bypasses the HTTP cache
Day 8 · 28 Oct

A splash with style

  • Splash: a forklift drifting a figure eight with smoke, boxes and permanent skidmarks
  • Higher wall/floor contrast; the in-game forklift restyled to match the splash
  • Bigger levels now that the map pans
  • Fullscreen is an opt-in menu setting; pause-menu touches no longer reach the pad
  • Service worker auto-reloads on a new version
Day 8 · 28 Oct

New soundtrack & calmer menus

  • Music library replaced: country, classical, industrial metal, techno and more, all CC0 / public domain
  • Music keeps playing in the pause menu and when focus is lost
  • Tutorial restart moved from the H key to a pause-menu button
  • Pause-menu shortcuts for tutorial, resume, auto-fullscreen and hard mode dropped
Days 8–9 · 28 – 29 Oct

The cabin view, rebuilt

  • Cabin renderer rewritten as modular js/pov/, first with identical output
  • New forks and camera: the eye follows fork height and the view tilts with the forks
  • More factory fluff: AC units, toilet doors, deer sign, roller doors and slogan posters
  • Doorways with hazard thresholds, jamb posts and a header beam
  • Faster: occlusion and off-screen culling, batched floor decals
Days 9–10 · 29 – 31 Oct

Look windows & a calmer floor

  • Mirrors replaced by two small top-down look windows: just ahead of the forks and just behind the tail
  • Start stacks cluster in bays with a big yellow outline; fewer random floor decals, yellow kept out of doorways
  • Cabin zone labels are clipped by walls and boxes in front of them; longer decal draw distance
  • The static top-down floor is cached in an offscreen canvas
  • Touch easter egg: tap the minimap to swap overview and cab view
Day 12 · 1 Nov

Online scores & a testing site

  • Local high scores are uploaded to an online server; the HUD and a start board show local and global best
  • GitHub Pages workflow deploys main to the site and testing to /testing/
  • Everything except the production host gets a red TESTING banner and favicon; scores are only posted from production
  • Touch polish: centred two-row button labels, overview forced in portrait, pad ignored behind overlays
  • Backing box behind the fork level gauge
Days 12–13 · 1 – 2 Nov

Wonky stacks & sturdier levels

  • Fresh stacks start slightly crooked; crooked zone stacks get a popup and a sad wah-wah (an off-key success chime)
  • Level generator rejects levels with unreachable stacks or zones and retries the same level
  • Saves validate the full snapshot and persist the game clock
  • Dead-code audit; shared drawing for extruded box and forklift sides
  • 43 humorous slogan posters for the walls (a few on the next slide)

Day 15 · 4 Nov

Disco in the dark

  • Dark mode gets glitter balls and coloured light pools sweeping the floor, in the cabin view and (as lights only) the overview
  • Balls are scattered randomly per level; the forklift carries its own light
  • Menu options: disco lights on/off, and colour mode Follow OS / Light / Dark
Day 15 · 4 Nov

Renamed: Pallet Polka

  • The game became Pallet Polka: new title on the splash and wall posters, polka MIDI as default music
  • Cover image (SVG source + PNG), a short description text and a new production address, palletpolka.gnutt.se
  • Level start and end screens show the local and global top 10
Day 18 · 7 Nov

True 3D with WebGL2

  • The overview, cabin view and look windows are now real 3D scenes (depth buffer, lighting, fog), no libraries; the 2D renderers remain as a fallback (?gl=0)
  • Dark mode headlights, brake/reverse lights, an idle tail glow, ceiling lights and disco spots as real point lights
  • Richer forklift model: carriage plates, lift chains, mast head and cylinder rod
  • Wall decals and zone labels painted into the 3D overview
  • Damaged boxes crumple: dented lids, corners and sides, scaled by damage
Day 18 · 7 Nov

A new menu, splash & touch schemes

  • Pause menu redesigned: one option per row, submenus for sound, visuals, levels and state, primary actions on top
  • Short landscape phones get a two-column menu
  • Splash: Disco start button with a dancing man, a story blurb, random music; music and disco are off by default
  • Touch control schemes: classic buttons, rotated buttons, or a virtual joystick with fork buttons

Splash and joystick on the phone

Day 19 · 8 Nov

Chase camera & look around

  • Third-person chase camera for the overview (C, menu option or double tap): spring-mounted, orbits to the front when reversing and upwards when a wall hides the forklift
  • Drag to orbit the overview; W/Q or drag to look around in the cabin; camera zoom slider with reset
  • The overview cuts walls at a low height; the minimap shows the chase camera's floor footprint
  • D/R gear state drives reverse lights and beeper; a mirrored reversing-camera inset in the cabin
  • "Bare forks" option for an unobstructed cabin view
Day 19 · 8 Nov

Doorway flair & tidier walls

  • Doorways get hazard chevrons on the wall ends, amber beacons on the jamb posts and a glowing header lamp
  • Roller doors and window signs on outer walls only; no decals inside doorways; toilet doors stay off interior walls
  • GL overview walls use the cabin-view look; dark-mode ceiling spots with capped strength
  • Look windows separated in the touch landscape cabin view; ?skip URL parameter bypasses the splash
Days 20–21 · 9 – 10 Nov

Multiplayer: up to four trucks

  • Host a game from the splash or the in-game menu; friends join with a party code (WebRTC, host is authoritative)
  • Shared level and score; each truck has its own colour, gauge and lights
  • Trucks collide; hand over or split carried stacks with your tines
  • Guests predict their own truck, so high ping does not hurt driving
  • Menu roster with ping, kick and ban; sounds from other trucks fade with distance
  • Horn on H with floating HOOOONK labels
Days 20–21 · 10 – 11 Nov

Sparks, smoke & impact physics

  • GL particle effects: sparks on wall scrapes, tyre and damage smoke, dust and confetti
  • Tyre squeal while the wheels spin
  • Off-centre hits spin and skid the truck; stacks pivot, and fast impacts topple upper boxes onto nearby stacks
  • Multiplayer trucks pop in and out with smoke, stars and a pop sound
  • North compass and a minimap with truck arrows
Days 20–23 · 10 – 12 Nov

Endless mode: lorries & loading bays

  • New Start endless mode: lorries arrive at the incoming bays, you unload them and load the delivery trucks
  • Delivery lorries check the tines are clear, honk, then open and close their gates
  • Numbered bays with waiting times; the pace adapts to the measured unloading rate (and crew size)
  • Running score, autosave, local and global endless high scores; works in multiplayer
  • Lorries redesigned with roofs and closed doors; starry void (dark) or sky with clouds (light)
