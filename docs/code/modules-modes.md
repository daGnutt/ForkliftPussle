Bots use the unified `state` (replacing the old `activity`/curiosity/observing/yield fields). **flee** (blinking red; 1.2x forklift speed from a moving forklift, 1.25x from a right-of-way bot or a yell) when a moving forklift is near, another bot is in right-of-way (unless this bot outranks it), or a working bot yells at it; a fleeing tape bot drops its tape job. **right-of-way** (blue, 0.75x) is entered only from going-to-work when another bot blocks the way and lasts until the job is reached. **going-to-work** (amber, 0.75x), **working** (amber, 0.75x; tape laying 0.25x; a blocked worker yells in botspeak and forces the blocker to flee; fixBot maps `go` to going-to-work and `up/carry/down` to working), **loiter** (no beacon, 0.75x; seeks a spot that sees a forklift, far or by a corner; after 600 s without a job the bot retracts into its hatch). Interruptions save no state: the bot re-evaluates its jobs afterwards.

Bot routes account for other visible bots, except that a bot in right-of-way can pass through any other bot without either bot blocking or pushing the other. Lower-priority bots still flee from its right-of-way request.

Every worker stores a serializable `bot.task` containing its job key, identity and destination snapshot. Selection and multi-target route searches run only while uncommitted; target movement or disappearance is checked at the destination, not while travelling. Cleaning and inspection retain the task while working; cone and tape tasks clear when their spawn/despawn or stage is committed, and the righting bot commits each movement phase separately. A bot clears its task when fleeing a forklift or yielding, when a route is skipped, or when arrival validation fails; it searches again on a subsequent tick. Right-of-way waiting and passing retain the task. Skip timers prevent immediate reacquisition, including when no other job exists. A blocked missing tape post may be abandoned and relocated to a reachable cell before selecting a new task.

The righting bot (`makeFixBot`/`stepFixBot`) also lives behind the hatch and works while any toppled box (`pen.tip`/`pen.loose`) exists. `pickFixJob` ranks jobs by tier, using shortest navigable route distance within each tier: reachable pallet (0), reachable box (1), then the same two as cheats (2, 3; reachable = the planner's nearest cell is within `FIX_REACH` of the target, otherwise it beams from the nearest point). Phases: `go` (drives collapsed) → `up` (unfolds, beams the object up) → `carry` (drives unfolded to a `freeSpot`, or for a box to its pallet's pose) → `down` (sets it down; a box is only ever set down right beneath the bot (the cheat ray pulls its pallet in under it too), and free spots keep 2.5 tiles from the hatch; a pallet gets `loose.set`, a box is placed upright onto it with `tip`/`loose` removed, damage kept). It gives up on a job after `FIX_GIVE_UP` seconds. When it flees, the righting bot drops any carried object at its current position and abandons the job. The same hatch houses the cone and tape bots (`makeConeBot`/`stepConeBot`, `makeTapeBot`/`stepTapeBot`). Cone targets follow oil spills and individually inspected completed stack corners, excluding corners close to another drop point's corner or inside an intact taped area; when an area is taped off, the cone bot retrieves cones from inside it. `stepConeCollisions` lets trucks and stacks displace, tip or squash cones before the cone bot restores them. Bots use a shared stuck watchdog: lack of movement for `STUCK_SECS` builds an escape route away from the job, and repeated failures temporarily skip the current job key so the bot can try another job or return later. Bots compare outstanding jobs by shortest navigable grid route (not straight-line Euclidean distance), so walls, stacks and temporary obstacles do not make a geometrically nearby job win. Retrieved cones go straight into unlimited onboard storage; the cone bot follows forklifts between jobs. `g.tapes` stores area outlines, a legacy signature field, four posts and four segments; `stepTapeCollisions` breaks individual pieces against forklift and box footprints. The narrow tape bot plans around actual box footprints. If a wall leaves too little room beside an area, its corner posts are placed against the wall and that side is left untaped. The bot visits successive corners, pulling an unfinished segment from its rear (`trailingTape`); it retries interrupted work and repairs physical damage only after all stacks have current successful inspections. A segment interrupted by a box or other footprint gets a short per-edge retry cooldown so the bot cannot immediately redraw the same broken line in a loop. If a missing post target makes no progress for 3 s, the bot moves that post to the nearest reachable safe grid cell; `postPoints` stores the adjusted corner so the outline, collision and rendering all agree and the location survives saves/party sync. Spawn aiming retains the selected heading while turning to avoid a rotating target. The bot's rear doors open only while drawing tape (`tapeDeploying`); it travels at 1 tile/s while drawing, 2 while travelling for work, and 1.5 while curious or returning home; fleeing and hatch movement retain their doubled speed. The 2D, GL and software cabin views draw the tracked white/yellow bot, beacon posts and elevated caution tape; `TAPE_BOT_PARTS` shares the 3D bot geometry. The tape bot installs or repairs an outline only once every drop point is valid and has a current successful individual inspector scan. It removes an outline immediately if a drop point becomes invalid, or when any current scan reports wonkiness or repairable damage; while scans are pending, valid outlines remain in place and repairs wait. If the area becomes valid again before the bot reaches the committed post, removal is cancelled on arrival. The inspector still rescans individual changed stacks. Obsolete outlines are dismantled pole by pole, with each removed pole taking its two attached tape segments with it. `areaComplete` uses exact `zoneDone` plus placement and repairable-damage checks.

The cone bot also frames every settled toppled box/barrel (`pen.tip`) and loose pallet (`pen.loose`) with four corner cones (`hazard` in `coneTargets`), retrieved once the righting bot has fixed them. The cone and tape bots finish the current stack point or tape area before switching; when no work remains at that point/area, they select the nearest outstanding job. If a cone target is blocked, the cone bot places a flagged cone on the nearest reachable clear floor instead. It retries misplaced cones every 10 seconds when other work permits, placing an improved cone before retrieving the old one; unreachable retrievals are temporarily skipped until their retry timer expires. The cone bot resembles a small white-and-red three-wheeled delivery vehicle with a dark face, warm eyes and an amber lightbar. `CONE_BOT_PARTS` and `CONE_BOT_WHEELS` describe its shared GL/software-cabin geometry; the 2D overview draws a matching top-down version.

`BOT_BEACONS` shares the four bots' roof-lamp geometry. `botBeaconOn` blinks at 2 Hz from `state`: red in flee, blue in right-of-way, amber in going-to-work/working, off in loiter or hidden.
